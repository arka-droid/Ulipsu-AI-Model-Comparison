Evaluating AI Models For SRT Translation

Ft. ILMs

Comprehensive tracking of pedagogical utility, reading constraints (CPS), and academic register across Gemini, GPT-5.5, Minimax, Gemma, GPT-4o, and Sarvam AI for Interactive Learning Modules.

Audit Scope & Methodology

Audit Scope

This audit strictly evaluates Large Language Models (LLMs) on their capability to generate Hindi SubRip Subtitle (SRT) files intended specifically for Interactive Learning Modules (ILMs). It does not assess general conversational AI fluency or creative prose translation. The focus is rigidly on educational utility and screen-reading technical constraints.

Evaluation Methodology

Models were empirically scored out of 100 based on an aggregated analysis of five parameters:

Pedagogical Scaffolding
Bridges Hindi text with source English concepts.
Academic Register
Maintains formal, age-appropriate vocabulary suitable for education.
CPS Optimisation
Minimises character count to support subtitle readability at standard frame rates.
Syntactic Accuracy
Ensures grammatical correctness and natural Hindi sentence structure.
Semantic Accuracy
Preserves the meaning, intent, and instructional value of the source text.

Evidence-Based Comparison

Domain Terminology & Scaffolding

'We learned how open letters help express your ideas clearly and how petitions show public support.'

Set C (Gemini 3)

Winner

'Humne seekha ki khula patra (open letter) aapke vichaaron ko spasht roop se vyakt karne mein kaise madad karta hai...'

Perfect educational design. Uses brackets to link the Hindi translation back to the English source terminology.

Set F (GPT-4o)

Pure Hindi

'Humne seekha ki khule patra aapki baat ko spasht roop se vyakt karne mein kaise madad kar sakte hain...'

Provides a highly fluent pure Hindi translation. Identical in approach to Set B, bypassing educational scaffolding entirely.

Set G (Sarvam)

Pure Hindi

'Humne seekha ki kaise khule patra aapko apne vichaaron ko spasht roop se vyakt karne mein madad kar sakte hain...'

Performs equally well as premium models here. Delivers a flawless, pure Hindi translation without grammar or logic issues.

Set A (GPT 5.5)

Rejected

'Humne seekha ki kaise open letters aapko apne vicharon ko...'

Relies heavily on transliteration. Assumes the student already comprehends the English vocabulary.

Academic Register & Tone Appropriateness

'A respectful tone, a clear message, evidence to support your argument, a direct call to action, and a touch of politeness.'

Set B (GPT 5.5+)

Winner

'Ek sammanjanak swar, ek spasht sandesh, apne tark ke samarthan mein pramaan...'

Superb academic balance. Selects 'pramaan', preserving a formal yet entirely accessible educational register.

Set G (Sarvam)

Runner-Up

'Ek sammaanjanak lahja, ek spasht sandesh, aapke tark ka samarthan karne ke liye pramaan...'

Matches the premium models perfectly. Selects correct terminology ('pramaan') while maintaining an exceptional structural flow.

Set E (Minimax)

Hyper-Formal

'Ek sammaanajanak svar, ek spasht sandesh, aapke tark ka samarthan karne ke liye saakshya...'

Employs highly formal vocabulary ('saakshya'), unnecessarily elevating the text to a legal standard and increasing cognitive load.

Set D (Gemma)

Colloquial Flaw

'...jungle mein koode-karkat jaisi ek saadharan chinta...'

Drops into colloquial slang ('koode-karkat'), breaking the formal academic tone required for Interactive Learning Modules.

The Flaw of AI Literalism

'A respectful tone, a clear message, evidence to support your argument, a direct call to action, and a touch of politeness.'

Set B (GPT 5.5+)

Winner

'...kaarvaai ke liye seedha aahvaan aur shishtata ka sparsh.'

Demonstrates brilliant handling of idioms. Translates 'touch of politeness' idiomatically as 'shishtata ka sparsh', maintaining academic dignity.

Set G (Sarvam)

Runner-Up

'...kaaryavaahi ke liye ek seedha aahvaan aur vinamrata ka sparsh.'

Superb native translation. Captures 'touch of politeness' effortlessly as 'vinamrata ka sparsh', completely avoiding literal traps.

Set C (Gemini 3)

Capable

'...seedha kaarvaai ka aahvaan aur vinamrata ka sparsh.'

Uses 'vinamrata ka sparsh'. Similar to Set B and G, it correctly adapts the idiom without sounding robotic or physically literal.

Set F (GPT-4o)

Literal Flaw

'...kaarvaai ka ek seedha aahvaan aur ek sparshbhari vinamrata.'

CRITICAL FLAW: 'Sparshbhari' literally implies 'full of physical touch'. Translating the idiom literally results in an awkward phrase.

Executive Insight & Economics

Projected API scaling costs for enterprise deployment. Translation only. Whisper transcription excluded.

Strategic Localisation at Scale

When selecting a Large Language Model for enterprise-scale Interactive Learning Modules, linguistic precision must be balanced against unit economics. Our audit definitively positions Gemini 3 as the optimal solution. It uniquely integrates essential pedagogical scaffolding natively into its output, while remaining exceptionally cost-effective.

While GPT 5.5+ and GPT-4o exhibit extraordinary syntactic fluency, they operate at a substantial pricing premium. They are best reserved for high-stakes, low-volume translation environments rather than automated, mass-pipeline generation.

The Sarvam AI Paradox

While Sarvam AI through its translation API calls is a cost-effective Indic-native model, but it fails the rigorous academic audit. Despite producing moments of excellent syntactic fluency, it exhibits severe grammatical flaws (e.g., incorrect pronoun casing) and inappropriate code-switching—randomly inserting untranslated English phrases like 'call to action' into Hindi sentences. This destroys the formal register required for ILMs. Consequently, the human-in-the-loop editing effort required to correct these unpredictable errors negates its initial API cost advantages, making it unviable for automated production pipelines.
